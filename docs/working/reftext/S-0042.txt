ABSTRACT
Purpose: To develop a personalised course recommendation system that automatically
suggests relevant courses based on users’ specific queries and interests, thereby improving
learning outcomes and user satisfaction.
Design/Methodology/Approach: The system leverages a Learning-to-Rank (LTR)
framework using XGBoost to model the relationship between user queries and course content.
The raw dataset comprised a total of 2.3 million records, consisting of 1,000,000 records from
Coursera, 900,000 from Udemy, and 400,000 from edX. These records span 12 major subject
categories (e.g., Computer Science, Business, Data Science, Health Sciences) and three
difficulty levels (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced). At the Learning-to-Rank stage, a
pairwise ranking strategy was implemented using XGBoost. Training instances were
constructed as course pairs (i,j) where course i was preferred over j based on past user
behaviour.
Findings: The performance is strong, with a Normalised Discounted Cumulative Gain
(NDCG) score of 0.83 and high Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) values. The system achieves
low query processing latency, ensuring real-time responsiveness. Post-retraining user feedback
shows a 90% improvement in user satisfaction, validating the adaptive nature of the approach.

INTRODUCTION
The rise of online learning platforms has brought about both opportunities and challenges. On
the one hand, learners today have access to thousands of courses in diverse subjects through
platforms such as Coursera, edX, and Udemy. On the other hand, this abundance of content
makes it difficult for individuals to select courses that truly align with their interests and goals.
In this context, personalised recommendation systems are becoming essential tools for
enhancing learning experiences and guiding students toward suitable content (Amin et al.,
2024; Schein,2002).
These systems aim to offer course suggestions by analysing user profiles, past interactions, and
course metadata (Tang, 2005; Brusilovsky, 2007). However, designing such systems for
educational environments comes with its own set of challenges. One of the most well-known
issues is the “cold start” problem, where the system lacks enough information about new users
or new courses to make accurate recommendations (Adomavicius, 2005; Islam & Hosen,
2025). Additionally, learners often change their preferences over time, making it necessary for
the system to adapt continuously to remain relevant.
Traditional methods, such as collaborative filtering and content-based filtering, have been
widely used in course recommendation; however, both have limitations. Collaborative filtering
tends to perform poorly when user-item interaction data is sparse (Xiaoyuan Su, 2009), while
content-based filtering can be too narrow, often recommending similar courses based on static
features, and failing to capture evolving user interests (Resnick,1997).
To address these issues, researchers have begun using Learning-to-Rank (LTR) models, which
are better suited for ranking tasks where the goal is not only to find relevant items but also to
order them based on importance or usefulness (Qin, 2010; Liu, 2007). Among these, XGBoost
has emerged as a strong candidate due to its efficiency, scalability, and ability to learn from
complex feature sets (Mrhar & Abik, 2025; Gbedawo et al., 2024; Chen, 2016). When

LITERATURE REVIEW
Recommendation systems have undergone significant development over the past two decades,
evolving from basic rule-based filtering to more advanced models that incorporate machine
learning and ranking algorithms. The success of any recommendation system largely hinges on
its ability to accurately capture user preferences and rank relevant content in a way that aligns
with individual needs.
Conventional techniques, such as collaborative filtering and content-based filtering, have been
extensively used in various domains. Collaborative filtering relies on past user-item behaviours
but is plagued by data sparsity as well as the cold-start issue, which restricts its power in serving
new users or courses (Su & Khoshgoftaar, 2009; Adomavicius & Tuzhilin, 2005). Content-
based filtering strategies, though efficient in utilising predefined item features, tend to be
inadequate in accommodating the dynamic evolution of users' preferences (Song, Shin, & Shin,
2024; Resnick & Varian, 1997). The hybrid strategies, as surveyed by Burke (2002), try to
integrate the advantages of various recommendation strategies. However, they tend to require
extensive manual tuning and lack the scalability needed for real-time deployment in dynamic
environments.
To address these challenges, Learning-to-Rank (LTR) methodologies have been proposed as a
more effective alternative. Specifically developed to tackle ranking tasks, LTR models can learn
to identify the optimal orderings of items from labelled data that reflect user relevance
judgments (Liu, 2009). Among these techniques, Chen and Guestrin (2016) demonstrated that

XGBoost exhibits remarkable performance because of its scalability, computational power, and
ability to model intricate feature interactions between user queries and course metadata. The
adoption of XGBoost in an LTR pipeline enables the direct optimisation of ranking metrics,
such as the Normalised Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG), thereby enhancing the quality
of personalised recommendations (Zhou & Wang, 2025).
Previous research has emphasised the benefits of LTR-based methods. For example, Qin et al.
(2010) and Joachims (2002) reported significant improvements in ranking performance through
pairwise and listwise ranking strategies. Furthermore, Zhang et al. (2019) highlighted the
importance of incorporating user feedback into the learning process, illustrating that real-time
responsiveness and personalisation can be considerably enhanced through adaptive model
updates.
Despite these advancements, many existing systems still struggle to update rankings
dynamically based on user interaction. This leads to a disconnect between theoretical progress
and real-world performance, particularly in fast-paced educational platforms. To address this
gap, the present study introduces a feedback-driven course recommendation framework
leveraging LTR with XGBoost. The proposed system aims to deliver scalable, real-time, and
highly personalised course suggestions that align recommendations with users’ evolving
learning objectives.

Data Sources and Coverage
Data was acquired through a combination of web scraping (using platform-compliant crawling

includes a comprehensive set of 15 attributes per course, capturing both static metadata and
dynamic engagement metrics. Static attributes include course title, description, category,
instructor details, difficulty level, duration, and pricing model. Dynamic attributes include user
ratings, reviews, enrollment counts, click-through rates (CTR), completion rates, and feedback
scores.
The raw dataset comprised a total of 2.3 million records: 1,000,000 from Coursera, 900,000
from Udemy, and 400,000 from edX. These records span 12 major subject categories (e.g.,
Computer Science, Business, Data Science, Health Sciences) and three difficulty levels
(Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced). This breadth ensures adequate representation of both
popular and niche courses, as detailed in Table 1.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The experimental evaluation was conducted on the curated multi-source dataset, detailed in this
section, which comprises approximately 3.2 million course entries aggregated from Coursera,
Udemy, and edX. The dataset encompasses a broad range of course metadata and user
interaction logs, spanning diverse subject areas and varying difficulty levels (beginner,
intermediate, and advanced). To ensure a fair evaluation, the data were split into 80% training
and 20% testing sets using stratified sampling, thereby preserving the distribution of course
categories and engagement levels.
The proposed recommendation framework utilised the Learning-to-Rank (LTR) paradigm with
XGBoost in pairwise ranking mode (rank: pairwise objective). A comprehensive
hyperparameter tuning process was carried out using 5-fold cross-validation on the training set.
The model was optimised for Normalised Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG), which
emphasises the importance of placing highly relevant courses at the top of the recommendation
list. All Experimentation was executed on a workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon 3.6 GHz
CPU, 64 GB RAM, and NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU. The implementation leveraged Python
3.11, scikit‑learn, and the official XGBoost library and evaluated on Normalised Discounted
Cumulative Gain NDCG@K, the Discounted Cumulative Gain (DCG@K), and MRR (Mean
Reciprocal Rank).

Experimental Results

The Learning-to-Rank (LTR) framework based on XGBoost was fine-tuned using grid search
and 5-fold cross-validation, to optimise the NDCG metric on the validation set while preserving
generalisation ability. The model employed the rank: pairwise objective, tailored for pairwise
ranking tasks. A learning rate of 0.1 was used to enable gradual convergence, while maximum
tree depth was set to 8 to balance model complexity and training time. A total of 300 boosting
rounds were allowed, with early stopping triggered if performance plateaued for 20 consecutive
rounds. Additional regularisation was introduced through L1 (reg_alpha=0.1) and L2
(reg_lambda=1.0) penalties. Subsampling strategies row-wise (subsample=0.8) and column-
wise (colsample_bytree=0.7), were incorporated to reduce overfitting. Other settings included
min_child_weight=1 and gamma=0.2, which prevent unnecessary splits by enforcing split
constraints.

These hyperparameters collectively resulted in a well-regularised, high-performing model that
exhibited strong ranking performance with minimal computational overhead. The use of early
stopping and subsampling aligns with prior findings by Chen and Guestrin (2016), who
emphasised these techniques as critical for maintaining model generalisation in gradient-
boosting frameworks. Similarly, the pairwise ranking objective aligns with the

responsiveness. Moreover, the model’s low latency aligns with Tang and McCalla (2005), who

results not only validate the proposed framework’s technical soundness but also reinforce the

Effect of the Feedback Loop
The role of the feedback mechanism was examined by measuring model performance in two
operational modes: one that leveraged real-time user interactions (clicks, enrollments, ratings),
and the other that operated statically without feedback. Incorporating dynamic feedback
significantly enhanced recommendation quality, with NDCG@10 improving by 9% (from 0.78
to 0.85), and MRR increasing by 11% (from 0.73 to 0.81). These improvements demonstrate
the model’s ability to dynamically adjust rankings in response to evolving user preferences,
supporting prior findings by Zhang et al. (2019) and Joachims (2002) that user-interaction data
play a critical role in refining model personalisation and responsiveness. This adaptive
behaviour aligns with the principles discussed by Brusilovsky and Millán (2007), who
emphasised that real-time adaptability is vital in educational recommender systems to sustain
learner engagement.

McCalla (2005) that adaptive, feedback-driven recommendation systems are essential for
maintaining learner engagement and improving user satisfaction in dynamic e-learning
environments.

the XGBoost algorithm to deliver real-time, personalised course recommendations in dynamic
e-learning environments. By embedding a real-time feedback loop, the proposed system
continuously refines its ranking model based on user interactions such as clicks, enrollments,
and ratings, enabling it to adapt seamlessly to evolving learner preferences. Leveraging a diverse

user embeddings, and engagement-driven features for fine-grained ranking.
Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed feedback-enhanced LTR model
substantially outperforms traditional collaborative filtering and content-based approaches across
all key performance metrics, achieving an NDCG@10 of 0.85, MRR of 0.81, and Precision@10
of 0.83, while maintaining a low average latency of 112 milliseconds. These findings confirm
that integrating LTR with XGBoost not only enhances ranking precision but also ensures real-
time responsiveness—an essential attribute for large-scale educational platforms.
The feedback mechanism proved particularly influential, yielding measurable improvements in
adaptability and user satisfaction, thereby reinforcing conclusions from prior research that
highlight the importance of interaction-driven learning in recommendation systems. Overall, the
research highlights the importance of integrating advanced ranking algorithms with continuous
user feedback to deliver accurate, context-aware, and scalable course recommendations. The
proposed framework provides a generalizable foundation for enhancing user engagement and
learning outcomes across varied educational contexts.
