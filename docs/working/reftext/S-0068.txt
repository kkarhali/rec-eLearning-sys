          Abstract: This paper proposes a dynamic privacy preserving intelligent
          recommendation method under a joint learning framework to address the
          contradiction between privacy protection and recommendation accuracy caused
          by cross platform data silos in English learning recommendations. Firstly, this
          paper conducts an in-depth analysis of multimodal English learning behaviour
          and uses transformers to capture cross modal semantic associations. Secondly,
          this paper innovatively designs a data sensitivity evaluation mechanism based
          on Kullback Leibler (KL) divergence to achieve dynamic allocation of
          differential privacy budget. Finally, this paper proposes a federated attention
          mechanism to achieve cross platform user interest transfer. The experiment
          shows that this method increases the hit rate by 37% in cold start scenarios,
          reduces communication overhead by 76%, and controls the success rate of
          member inference attacks at 0.13, effectively solving the collaborative
          optimisation problems of privacy security, recommendation accuracy, and
          communication efficiency in cross platform English learning recommendations.

          Keywords: dynamic privacy protection; English learning behaviour analysis;
          cross-platform recommendation; intelligent recommendation system; federated
          learning.

1   Introduction

With the acceleration of digital transformation of education, the field of English learning
has accumulated a massive amount of multi-source heterogeneous user behaviour data
(Susanty et al., 2021; Nugroho and Atmojo, 2020; Soyoof et al., 2023). The personalised
recommendation system based on big data has significantly improved the learning
efficiency through the unique learning trajectory of English (such as vocabulary
forgetting curve, grammatical error clustering pattern), knowledge mastery and
interaction mode (Lahiassi et al., 2023; Raj and Renumol, 2022; Khanal et al., 2020).
However, existing systems face dual challenges in cross-platform collaborative
recommendation: on the one hand, over-reliance on centralised data aggregation leads to
a surge in the risk of sensitive data leakage, such as oral practice recordings and writing
content, and the constraints on data circulation imposed by regulations such as the EU
General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) exacerbate the problem of data silos
between platforms (Javeed et al., 2023; Ogunseyi et al., 2023; Chronis et al., 2024). On
the other hand, the traditional differential privacy protection mechanism adopts a static
noise injection strategy, which seriously damages the accuracy of diagnosing language
skill weaknesses while ensuring privacy security (Feng et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025),
especially in the cold start scenario, the conversion rate of new users drops by more than
30%. This inherent contradiction between privacy protection and recommendation utility
has become a key bottleneck restricting the development of educational recommendation
systems (Da Silva et al., 2023).
    For English learning scenarios, this study needs to solve three core problems: first,
the feature fusion and sensitivity quantification of multimodal learning behaviour data
(such as vocabulary retrieval frequency in click streams and grammatical error
distribution in answering questions) (Sharma and Giannakos, 2020); second, the dynamic
allocation mechanism of privacy budget for non-independent and identically distributed
(non-IID) data (such as differences in listening proficiency among users in different
regions) under the cross-platform federated learning framework (Zhang et al., 2024);
third, the communication efficiency bottleneck caused by real-time oral practice
streaming in large-scale distributed training (Wang et al., 2024). These issues directly
affect the educational value of the system: multimodal modelling determines the depth of
cognition of the laws of language skill transfer (such as the relationship between reading
ability and writing), and the privacy mechanism affects the accuracy of grammar
correction recommendations for cold-start users (Jangid and Kumar, 2025). Existing
solutions generally have defects such as large feature alignment deviation, coarse privacy
protection granularity, and high communication overhead when dealing with these
problems (Yang and Li, 2025). Existing solutions generally have defects such as
large feature alignment deviation, coarse privacy protection granularity, and high
communication overhead when dealing with these problems. Although the multimodal

fusion method proposed by Cao et al. (2022) improves the recommendation classification
performance, it fails to cover the privacy protection requirements of highly sensitive data
such as writing. Chen et al. (2023) used the k-randomised response (kRR) mechanism to
introduce a dynamic privacy budget strategy in multidimensional data collection.
However, since the complexity of behavioural semantics in educational scenarios was not
considered, it was easy to cause language feature distortion. Ali et al. (2024) built a
blockchain-enabled asynchronous federated recommendation framework. Although it
enhanced the traceability of the model, the semantic consistency and interest migration
support in federated recommendation were insufficient, which may cause fluctuations in
recommendation results. These shortcomings reflect that educational recommendation
systems urgently need to coordinate privacy, semantic modelling, and collaborative
efficiency at the system level.
     In response to the cross-platform recommendation problem, Zeng et al. (2025)
proposed a pioneering cross-platform federated group recommendation (FedGR)
system. FedGR combines hypergraph convolution, attention aggregation, and fully
connected fusion components with federated learning. In terms of privacy protection,
Huang et al. (2023) proposed an AutoRec++ (autoencoder for recommendation)
model to comprehensively address various biases in user behaviour data. Regarding
communication optimisation, Chen et al. (2022) designed a gradient descent method with
sparsification and error correction (GD-SEC) to improve communication efficiency in a
general worker-server architecture. Gong et al. (2023) proposed a model contract that
includes preconditions and postconditions of pre-trained models to achieve better model
reuse. Current methods generally handle privacy protection, feature modelling, and
communication efficiency issues separately (Jegadeesan et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021), and
fail to solve the problem of feature migration for cold-start users.
     This study aims to establish a collaborative optimisation paradigm for privacy
protection, recommendation accuracy, and communication efficiency. First, drawing on
the experience of Wu et al. (2025) in integrating digital cognitive behavioural therapy
with eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing in medicine, it innovatively
constructs a multimodal data sensitivity assessment matrix to dynamically increase the
noise injection intensity of high-frequency sensitive data and increase the privacy budget
release rate of low-sensitivity data. Secondly, this paper integrates the construction and
application of professional knowledge graph based on educational big data by Wu and Jia
(2022), designs transformer-bidirectional long short-term memory (Bi-LSTM) hybrid
network, and uses transformer to capture cross-modal semantic associations. By
modelling temporal behaviour patterns through Bi-LSTM, this paper combines the
review results of knowledge graph embedding model by Gesese et al. (2021). At the
federated framework level, inspired by the federated learning model of the cross-context
recommendation system based on the self-attention mechanism proposed by Singh et al.
(2023), a federated attention mechanism is designed to dynamically adjust the knowledge
transfer weight by calculating the similarity of cross-platform user interests, thereby
improving the feature matching accuracy of new users in the cold start phase.
Finally, drawing on the algorithm technology of Cui et al. (2020) that applied the
blockchain-assisted federated learning compression algorithm to content caching, the
top-k gradient prediction algorithm is introduced to dynamically select important
parameters based on the historical update direction, reducing the communication
bandwidth overhead by 76% while maintaining the model convergence stability.
Different from pure technical research, the goal of this paper is to achieve in-depth

Ti is defined as a normalised soft-maximisation weight adjustment factor that
simultaneously responds to the local sample size imbalance and the discrepancy between
local and global gradient distributions. We define Di as a weighted combination of local
imbalance and distribution discrepancy, where Di equals alpha multiplied by the sample
size imbalance term plus beta multiplied by the gradient distribution discrepancy term.
Here, the sample size imbalance term is calculated as the difference between the
logarithm of the local sample size and the mean of the logarithms of the global sample
sizes, normalised by the sample standard deviation of the logarithms of the sample sizes.
The gradient distribution discrepancy term employs the Kullback-Leibler (KL)
divergence between the local gradient distribution and the global average gradient
distribution, with momentum estimation introduced to suppress fluctuations in

mini-batches. Let s be the temperature adjustment coefficient; then, Ti is defined as
exp(s ∗ Di) divided by the sum of exp(s ∗ Dj) for all clients j. According to this definition,
when the local sample size is extremely small or the local distribution significantly
deviates from the global distribution, Ti will relatively amplify the aggregation weight of
that client. Conversely, when the distributions of all clients are similar, Ti will approach
the standard weight based on sample size. The relevant hyperparameters alpha, beta, and
s are provided with candidate ranges in the methodology section and determined through
grid search on a validation set.
    To improve the participation of low-sample clients, the aggregation results are then
sent to the client through the same secure channel to keep the model synchronised.
Subsequent incremental aggregation is performed every 30 minutes to integrate new
behavioural features in real time. Integrity verification and retransmission mechanisms
are embedded in the communication process. If packet loss is detected, differential
retransmission is triggered to ensure that the training process is continuous and traceable.
The entire architecture achieves dual protection of model performance and data privacy
in cross-platform collaborative training through protocol layer encryption and dynamic
weight adjustment of the aggregation layer. The parameter transmission format is shown

2.2 Dynamic privacy protection mechanism
The dynamic allocation strategy of differential privacy budget quantifies the sensitivity of
client data based on KL divergence. To correct the bias introduced by gradient sparsity in
KL divergence estimation, this paper introduces a sparsity correction term to the original
KL estimate. The corrected sensitivity estimate is defined such that the corrected KL
value equals the original KL value plus lambda multiplied by the sparsity correction term.
Here, sparsity is defined as the proportion of elements in the local gradient vector with
absolute values less than a threshold theta. To reduce the impact of instantaneous
fluctuations on the estimation, the sparsity correction term is accumulated using
exponential smoothing and the threshold theta is initialised based on short-term statistics
from the early stages of training. The correction coefficient lambda is determined through
grid search on a validation set. The corrected KL value is used as the input to the privacy
budget mapping function, replacing the uncorrected KL value directly used in the original
text. This correction effectively reduces distribution estimation bias when gradients
exhibit zero-inflation or high sparsity, thereby avoiding unreasonable privacy budget
allocation based on unstable estimates. Before each round of update, the KL divergence
of the local gradient distribution Pi of the client i and the global average gradient
distribution PG is calculated:

2.4 Generation of federated recommendation strategy
The client inputs the fused learning behaviour feature vector into a two-layer fully
connected network [128 hidden units, activation function rectified linear unit (ReLU)],
and outputs a 256-dimensional interest vector zi ∈ R256. To clarify the rationale and
impact of kernel function selection in the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) loss,
this study supplements the methodology section with a kernel function selection and
comparison process. The candidate kernel functions selected include the Gaussian radial
basis function (RBF) kernel, the polynomial kernel, and the Laplacian kernel. For the
Gaussian kernel, the median of pairwise sample distances is used to initialise the
bandwidth, which is then fine-tuned via grid search on the validation set. For the
polynomial kernel, the optimal combination of polynomial degree and constant term is
searched on the validation set. For the Laplacian kernel, the same distance metric as the
Gaussian kernel is employed to ensure comparability. In the experimental setup,
empirical MMD reductions are computed for each of the three kernel functions, while
recommendation metrics for downstream tasks, such as Precision@Top 10 and
normalised discounted cumulative gain (NDCG)@Top 10, are also evaluated. Based on
these metrics, the kernel function that demonstrates optimal performance under the given
data heterogeneity is selected. The server measures the deviation between the client
interest distribution and the target distribution based on the MMD loss, and its loss

reading and writing. Among them, there are 9.8 million oral practice records and
6.2 million grammar correction data, indicating that oral and writing are high-frequency
items in user learning behaviour. To complete the processing pipeline for oral practice
records, this study establishes unified definitions and parameter settings for speech and
text modalities during the data preprocessing stage. All audio files are uniformly
resampled to a 16,000 Hz, mono, 16-bit PCM format. For each speech segment,
pre-emphasis filtering is first applied, followed by the computation of the short-time
Fourier transform (STFT) using a window length of 25 milliseconds and a step size of
10 milliseconds. The filtered energy spectrum is then obtained through a 40-dimensional
Mel filter bank, from which 13-dimensional Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients
228       S. Yang and D. Hao

(MFCCs) are calculated, along with their first and second-order differential terms. Prior
to training, each speech segment undergoes mean-variance normalisation on a per-frame
basis for each sample. For speech segments, voice activity detection is first performed to
remove silent segments using an energy threshold method, and a pre-trained end-to-end
speech recogniser is employed to generate text transcriptions along with confidence
scores. The transcriptions are aligned with timestamps through forced alignment, and the
confidence scores are used as inputs for subsequent privacy budget allocation and feature
weighting. The feature-engineered speech vectors are aligned to the model’s dimensions
through mini-batch normalisation and dimensionality reduction mapping before entering
the model. For the text modality, standardised tokenisation and subword segmentation are
performed using the same tokeniser as the recommendation model to map the text into a
sequence of token IDs, with subword-level embedding initialisation. Spelling or
recognition errors in oral text are annotated with confidence labels and treated as an
uncertainty feature in the model input. The distribution type is long-tail and non-IID,
accounting for about 70%, showing significant differences in data volume and behaviour

addition, the number of clients is 200, and each client simulates the learning behaviour of
400 users on average. With an average learning time of 24.6 minutes per round, the
federated learning setting in a cross-end heterogeneous environment is truly restored.

3.3 Quantitative verification of privacy protection strength
In the member inference attack experiment, three shadow models are trained for each
client, and the shadow model parameters are generated using the same federated process.
For each test record, the shadow model inputs behavioural features to generate predicted
probabilities and compares them with the output of the real attack target model. An attack
discriminator is constructed based on the cross-entropy difference to classify whether it is
a member of the training data. The attack success rate (ASR) is calculated as the ratio of
the number of correctly identified member samples to the total number of member
samples; the privacy leakage risk is evaluated by ASR and false alarm rate.
    Budget consumption monitoring uses moments accountant technology to record the ε
budget usage after each round of noise injection and generate a budget consumption
curve. This paper sets the threshold εmax = 1. If the cumulative amount εmax = 1, the
subsequent training rounds are stopped, and the convergence rounds are recorded to
compare the budget consumption rate and task completion under different budget
allocation strategies.
    In the experiment, the ASR difference between the fixed budget strategy and the
dynamic allocation strategy is compared. This verification process accurately quantifies
the defence effect and budget utilisation efficiency of the privacy protection mechanism
in actual attack scenarios. The comparison of attack success rate and privacy budget
consumption is shown in Figure 5.
    Figure 5 shows the comparison of attack success rate (ASR) and cumulative privacy
budget consumption under dynamic and fixed privacy budget strategies. The ASR of the
dynamic strategy drops rapidly from the initial to 0.13 in 50 rounds; the ASR of the fixed
strategy drops more volatile. Regarding the cumulative privacy budget consumption of
the dynamic and fixed strategies, the dynamic strategy has consumed ε = 0.54 in
20 rounds, while the fixed strategy only consumes ε = 0.40, but the ASR drop of the
dynamic strategy is significantly higher than that of the fixed strategy. The dotted line
marks the threshold of εmax =1, indicating that the dynamic strategy achieves better
privacy protection effect under the premise of not exceeding the budget limit.
    To quantify the impact of privacy protection on recommendation quality, the
recommendation performance of dynamic privacy mechanism and non-privacy federated
learning under the same experimental conditions is compared. The privacy-utility
trade-off comparison is shown in Table 6.
    Table 6 shows that when the privacy budget ε = 1, the dynamic mechanism reduces
Precision@10 by only 4.3%, while static differential privacy (ε = 1) reduces
Precision@10 by 12.4% (0.409), proving that the dynamic strategy significantly
alleviates the damage of privacy protection to precision.
    To comprehensively evaluate the robustness of the proposed privacy-preserving
mechanism, this experiment not only employs attack success rate (ASR) as the primary
metric but also introduces the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve
(AUC-ROC) as a supplementary evaluation metric. AUC-ROC comprehensively
measures the attack model’s ability to distinguish between member and non-member
samples across different thresholds. A value closer to 0.5 indicates better privacy
protection (i.e., poorer discriminatory capability of the attack model), while a value closer
to 1.0 suggests a higher risk of privacy leakage. By combining ASR and AUC-ROC, we
can more rigorously quantify the strength of privacy protection from two dimensions:
attack success rate at a single threshold and overall discriminatory capability.

During the federated training process, the client records the amount of local uploaded
gradient data and transmission delay before each round of aggregation: the completion
time of a single client gradient upload is measured under a 1 Gbps bandwidth restriction
environment, and the average value of each client is taken as the single-round delay
indicator. The server records the peak bandwidth usage after receiving all client gradients
in each round. To evaluate the robustness of the top-k transmission mechanism under
real-world unstable network conditions, a network jitter simulation experiment was

conducted. For this test, the baseline 200 Mbps inter-node bandwidth was dynamically
perturbed using a Gaussian noise model (mean = 0, standard deviation = 50 Mbps),
simulating sudden bandwidth drops and spikes. Additionally, the latency range was
expanded from 15–60 ms to 15–200 ms with a uniform random distribution to mimic
severe packet queuing delays. Under this jittery environment, the ‘transmission success
rate’ was measured as the percentage of global aggregation rounds in which all

convergence round stops when the global model no longer improves Precision@10 by

3.5 Cross-platform collaborative gain verification
For cold start users, 10% of all users are randomly selected as the experimental group.
Only the five earliest behaviour records of each user are retained for local model
initialisation, and the remaining behaviours are used as the test set. To examine the
sensitivity of the initial record count to the effectiveness of cold-start recommendations,
this study supplemented multiple sets of controlled experiments with varying initial
record counts in the cold-start experiments. The initial record counts were set to
five groups: 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20. Experiments were repeated under the same random seed
to ensure result comparability. For each group of experiments, we recorded the cold-start
hit rate (Precision@Top 10), NDCG@Top 10, the proportion of privacy budget
consumption, and the success rate of membership inference attacks. Paired statistical tests
were conducted to analyse performance differences across varying initial record counts,
utilising Student’s t-test and reporting p-values and confidence intervals. The results were
presented in tabular form and performance curves were plotted against changes in the
initial record count to quantify the impact of the initial record count on recommendation
effectiveness and privacy consumption. After each round of global aggregation, the client
generates a top-10 recommendation list based on the local initialisation model and the
latest global model, and matches the list with the first behaviour in the test set. If the first
behaviour course appears in the recommendation list, it is recorded as a successful
conversion.

3.6 Dynamic mechanism adaptability test
The sensitivity monitoring module maintains a recent gradient batch window of length
W = 50 on each client, and uses the KL divergence between the gradient distribution of
each batch in the sliding window and the gradient distribution of the previous window,
that is, ΔKL, as the sensitivity change. Whenever ΔKL > τ (τ = 0.05), a privacy budget
redistribution is triggered. The trigger time t1 and the corresponding noise injection
parameter update completion time t2 are recorded, and the response delay δ = t2 – t1 is
calculated (both the average and the 95% quantile are recorded). The trigger event and δ
value are output in the federated training log throughout the process.
     After the global aggregation process is completed, the budget reallocation frequency
statistics summarise the number of triggers per client in units of hours to obtain the
trigger frequency fi(t) for hour t. To account for the continuity of dynamic data changes,
the adjustment of the subsequent KL divergence threshold τ for each client is based on a
smoothed trigger frequency, calculated as a 3-hour moving average: fi,smooth(t) = (fi(t)
+ fi(t – 1) + fi(t – 2)) / 3. The threshold τ is then adaptively adjusted according to
fi,smooth(t): when fi,smooth(t) > fmax (fmax = 5 times/hour), τ is adjusted upward by 10%; when
fi,smooth(t) < fmin (fmin = 1 time/hour), τ is adjusted downward by 10%. This smoothing
mechanism ensures the system responds to sustained trends rather than transient spikes or
drops in sensitivity. The adaptively adjusted τ value is synchronously updated to the local
monitoring module. In the experimental environment, 50 rounds of federated training are
run continuously for 200 non-IID clients. The ‘privacy budget utilisation’ metric reported
236       S. Yang and D. Hao

in Table 6 represents the percentage of the total allocated privacy budget (ε = 1.0) that
was actually consumed by the system to complete its 50 training rounds. It is calculated
by dividing the cumulative privacy cost recorded by the moments accountant at the end
of training by the total budget limit (1.0), then multiplying by 100 to express it as a
percentage. A higher utilisation rate indicates that the mechanism is more efficient at
spending its privacy allowance to achieve model convergence, meaning it extracts more
utility from the same privacy guarantee. The performance comparison of the dynamic

4     Conclusions

This paper proposes an intelligent recommendation framework that integrates dynamic
privacy mechanism and federated learning to address the core contradiction between
privacy protection and recommendation utility in English learning recommendation
systems. In the speech modality, the phonological characteristics of different languages
and the availability of speech recognition resources will directly impact the quality of
       English learning behaviour analysis and intelligent recommendation system             237

oral features, thereby influencing dynamic privacy allocation and cold-start transfer
strategies. In the text and knowledge graph modalities, differences in writing systems and
the cross-lingual consistency of knowledge point mappings will affect the quality of
knowledge embeddings. Therefore, when applying the method to multilingual scenarios,
it is necessary to introduce multilingual pre-trained models or perform cross-lingual
alignment of knowledge graphs, and account for variations in automatic speech
recognition confidence scores and sparsity across different languages in privacy budget
mapping. The paper identifies these limitations and proposes directions for future work,
including conducting transfer experiments on multilingual public datasets to evaluate the
impact of adopting multilingual pre-trained models and cross-lingual knowledge
alignment on recommendation accuracy, privacy consumption and communication
efficiency. Related experimental plans are outlined as follow-up work to validate the
generalisation of this method in non-English environments. The KL divergence is used to
quantify the sensitivity of multimodal data, and the dynamic allocation of differential
privacy budget is realised. Combined with the adaptive Gaussian noise injection strategy,
the cold start hit rate is improved by 37% through language error pattern diagnosis and
knowledge point association recommendation while protecting user privacy. This paper
uses a hybrid model of transformer and Bi-LSTM to model learning behaviour temporal
features and cross-modal semantic associations, introduces knowledge graph embedding
technology to enhance feature representation capabilities, designs a federated attention
mechanism to achieve cross-platform interest migration, aligns distribution differences
through MMD loss, and uses top-k gradient compression and FedAdam optimiser
to collaboratively optimise communication efficiency, compress single-round
communication overhead, and improve convergence speed. Experimental verification
shows that this method effectively balances privacy security and recommendation
accuracy on a dataset of 80,000 users. The Precision@10 and NDCG@10 indicators
reach 0.447 and 0.394 respectively, and the attack success rate is controlled at 0.13,
providing a technical path that takes into account compliance and commercial value for
cross-platform collaborative recommendations in the field of education.
