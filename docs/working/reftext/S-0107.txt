Abstract: A system that is personalized and capable of automatically suggesting appropriate courses based on a user's
particular questions and interests provides customized recommendations. The system employs an LTR model relying on
XGBoost to learn relationships between the queries and the courses. Dynamic ranking feature refinement enhances
ranking, and a feedback loop constructs incrementally improving the recommendations by applying relevant courses to
update the model. The scraped educational sites are the foundation of the dataset, where there are granular course
descriptions as well as the interaction logs. Evaluation results indicate that the system is able to produce high ranking
outcomes as evidenced by an NDCG value of 0.85 and high values for MRR. The system is able to produce low query
processing latency, making it possible for real-time responsiveness. User feedback analysis following system retraining
indicated a 90% increase in user satisfaction. The suggested framework is dynamic and provides personalized
recommendations for courses in various learning environments.

Index Terms: Personalized Recommendation System, Learning-to-Rank, XGBoost, Course Ranking, User Feedback,
Educational Platforms.

1. Introduction

      The rapid growth of online learning platforms has created both significant opportunities and new challenges in
delivering personalized educational content. Learners currently have access to thousands of courses on various subjects
from websites such as Coursera, edX, and Udemy. On the other hand, this plethora of courses makes it hard for learners
to choose the ones that best suit their interests and aspirations. Here, personalized recommendation systems are
emerging as key tools for improving learning experiences and leading students to appropriate content [1].
      These systems work with a view to providing course recommendations based on user profiles, previous
interactions, and course information [2-3]. Creating such systems within educational contexts has its challenges. The
most popular of these is the "cold start" issue where the system does not have sufficient information regarding new
users or new courses upon which it can provide reliable recommendations [2]. Additionally, learners often change their
preferences over time, making it necessary for the system to adapt continuously to remain relevant.
      Classic approaches such as collaborative filtering and content-based filtering have been extensively utilized in
course recommendation, yet both of them are subject to limitations. Collaborative filtering is prone to low performance
when user-item interaction data is not dense [4], whereas content-based filtering is too restrictive and tends to
recommend similar courses due to static features, while neglecting the changing interest of users [5,6].
      To cater to these problems, researchers have begun employing Learning-to-Rank (LTR) models that are more
appropriate for ranking tasks where the objective is not only to identify relevant items but also to rank them in order
of importance or utility [7,8]. Of these, XGBoost has been a top contender because it is efficient, scalable, and can learn
from intricate feature sets [9]. When integrated with LTR methods, XGBoost can correctly represent user-user, query-
query, and course-course relationships to enhance the ranking of recommendations.
      Notably, new developments highlight the critical importance of feedback for enhancing recommendation quality.
Beyond the usage of historical data alone, systems can also be trained based on continued user behavior (e.g., clicks,
course completion, ratings) in order to dynamically refresh recommendations [10, 11]. Such a feedback loop renders the
recommendation engine more responsive and better able to cater to users in actual education contexts.
      In this paper, we present a feedback-driven recommendation framework based on Learning-to-Rank with XGBoost,
tailored for large-scale online education platforms. Our approach aims to deliver accurate, adaptive, and real-time
course suggestions, helping learners find the right content at the right time.
      This work is focused on

        How can Learning-to-Rank methods using XGBoost be applied to improve the ranking quality of course
         recommendations in e-learning platforms?
        What impact does a real-time feedback mechanism have on the personalization and adaptability of the
         recommendation system?
        How effective is the proposed system in terms of standard ranking metrics such as NDCG and MRR,
         compared to traditional recommendation techniques?

     The paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews prior related research and provides the motivation for the
current study. Section III provides a detailed overview of the proposed methodology. Section IV outlines the detailed
design and experimentation of the proposed model and results are presented in Section V, followed by the conclusion
and future scope.

2. Related Work

     Recommendation systems have undergone considerable development over the past two decades, evolving from
basic rule-based filtering to more advanced models incorporating machine learning and ranking algorithms. The success
of any recommendation system largely hinges on its ability to accurately capture user preferences and rank relevant
content in a way that aligns with individual needs.
     Traditional approaches such as collaborative filtering and content-based filtering have been extensively used in
many domains [15]. Collaborative filtering relies on past user-item interactions but is affected by data sparsity and the
cold-start problem, precluding its utility for new users or courses [4, 5, 19]. Techniques of content-based filtering,
though useful in making use of pre-defined item properties, tend to be unsatisfactory in responding to users' changing
tastes. This can lead to redundant, too-specific recommendations that do not capture evolving learning settings [6].
Hybrid strategies, including those surveyed by Burke [12], try to leverage the strengths of various recommendation
techniques. Unfortunately, they tend to demand heavy manual tuning and lack the scalability for real-time operations in
dynamic settings.
     To meet these challenges, Learning-to-Rank (LTR) models have been proposed as a stronger replacement. Tailored
to ranking tasks, LTR models can learn ideal item orderings from annotated datasets that reflect user relevance
judgments [7]. Of these approaches, XGBoost has proven to be highly effective because of its scalability, efficiency in
computations, and capacity to model complex feature interactions between course metadata and user queries [9]. The

incorporation of XGBoost as a component of an LTR pipeline facilitates end-to-end optimization of ranking scores like
the Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG), thus improving the accuracy of personalized suggestions [24].
     The advantages of LTR-based methods have been shown in the earlier research. Qin et al. [8] and Joachims [13]
showed great performance improvement in terms of ranking with pairwise and listwise ranking methods. Zhang et al.
[10] further highlighted the importance of using user feedback to improve the learning process by exhibiting important
improvements in real-time responsiveness and personalization through adaptive model updates [18].
     Recent advances in recommendation systems have increasingly focused on deep learning–based models that learn
complex user–item representations [14]. Survey studies highlight how neural architectures extend traditional
collaborative filtering by modeling nonlinear interactions and rich contextual features [21]. Neural recommender
models have demonstrated strong performance improvements in offline evaluations, particularly in dense interaction
environments [20]. More recent research has explored transformer-based and sequential recommendation models, which
leverage self-attention mechanisms to capture long-term dependencies in user behavior [22]. Such models have shown
promising results in sequential ranking tasks and personalized content delivery [23]. However, these approaches often
require large-scale interaction data, extensive hyperparameter tuning, and significant computational resources, which
may limit their applicability in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained environments. In spite of these developments,
most current systems are still lacking in dynamically refreshing rankings based on user activity. This creates a gap
between theoretical gains and actual performance, particularly in high-speed education platforms. To close this gap, this
work presents a feedback-driven course recommendation system based on LTR with XGBoost. The suggested system
seeks to provide scalable, real-time, and extremely personalized course recommendations, effectively mapping
recommendations to users’ changing learning objectives.

world, a wide range of academic and professional topics, and varied teaching approaches. This selection ensures that the
resulting dataset represents a broad range of learner profiles, content models, and interaction patterns offering a solid
basis for developing a generalized and scalable e-learning recommendation system.

synthetic user interaction simulation for personalization testing. The dataset includes a comprehensive set of 15
attributes per course, capturing both static metadata and dynamic engagement metrics (Table 1). Static attributes include

C. Discussion on Feature Representation Choice
     Recent advances in recommender systems increasingly favor embedding-based and transformer-based textual
representations due to their ability to capture deep semantic relationships. However, such models typically require
substantial computational resources and introduce higher inference latency, which may limit their practicality in large-
scale, interactive educational platforms.
     In this work, TF-IDF is employed as a deliberate and pragmatic feature representation. It offers a transparent and
computationally efficient means of modeling textual relevance between user queries and course descriptions. This
choice aligns with the real-time constraints of the proposed system, where low latency and scalability are essential.
Additionally, tree-based Learning-to-Rank models such as XGBoost are well suited to handling the sparse and high-
dimensional feature spaces produced by TF-IDF without requiring complex embedding training.
     TF-IDF is therefore not presented as a replacement for modern semantic embedding, but as a strong baseline that
balances effectiveness, interpretability, and deployment efficiency. The proposed framework is modular in design,
allowing transformer-based embedding to be incorporated in future extensions without altering the core ranking
architecture.
3.4. Learning to Rank with XGBoost
      In the Learning‑to‑Rank stage, a pairwise ranking strategy was implemented using XGBoost. XGBoost was
selected as the Learning-to-Rank model due to its strong empirical performance, scalability, and robustness in handling
heterogeneous feature sets. In the proposed framework, the ranking features include sparse textual representations,
behavioral signals, categorical attributes, and temporal features. Gradient-boosted decision tree models such as
XGBoost are well-suited for this setting, as they naturally handle mixed feature types, missing values, and nonlinear
feature interactions.
      Alternative Learning-to-Rank models, including LambdaMART and LightGBM, are widely used in large-scale
ranking systems and have demonstrated competitive performance in web search and recommendation tasks.
LambdaMART directly optimizes ranking-based loss functions, while LightGBM offers faster training through
histogram-based tree growth. However, prior comparative studies have shown that XGBoost achieves comparable
ranking effectiveness while providing stable convergence and flexible regularization controls, making it suitable for
iterative experimentation and feedback-driven updates. In this work, XGBoost was preferred due to its mature
ecosystem, ease of integration with incremental retraining pipelines, and reliable performance under moderate data
volumes.

3.5. Feedback Loop Integration
      To ensure adaptability and sustained relevance of recommendations, the system incorporates a dynamic feedback
loop that continuously refines model predictions based on evolving user behavior. This mechanism enables incremental
learning, where the model progressively improves by integrating recent user interactions. While Learning-to-Rank
algorithms optimize performance on historical data, real-world deployments require continual updates to reflect such as
shifting user interests, new courses added to the platform, and changing popularity trends. Feedback loops address this
need by incorporating explicit signals (e.g., ratings, reviews) and implicit signals (e.g., clicks, enrollments, time spent).

      To balance adaptability with computational efficiency, the feedback loop is implemented using a periodic
retraining strategy rather than continuous online updates. User interaction signals, including clicks, enrollments, ratings,
and time spent, are aggregated over fixed time windows and incorporated into the training dataset at scheduled intervals.
In the current implementation, daily model updates are applied for platforms with high user activity, while weekly
retraining is sufficient for environments with moderate interaction volumes. This approach ensures timely adaptation to
changing user preferences while maintaining model stability.
      The retraining cost is primarily influenced by the amount of newly accumulated feedback rather than the size of the
full dataset. To limit computational overhead, only incremental course–pair samples derived from recent user
interactions are appended during each update cycle. Based on the experimental setup described in Section 3, a single
retraining cycle required approximately 20–25 minutes, depending on feedback volume. Since retraining is performed
offline or during low-traffic periods, it does not interfere with real-time recommendation latency.
      From a scalability perspective, the feedback loop is designed to support growth in both user base and course
inventory. The retraining process can be parallelized across multiple processing cores or distributed computing
resources as data volume increases. Moreover, XGBoost efficiently handles sparse and high-dimensional feature
representations and supports parallel learning, allowing the system to scale linearly with the number of new feedback
instances. These characteristics make the proposed framework suitable for deployment in large-scale educational
platforms.

4. Results and Discussion

4.1. Experimental Setup
     The experimental evaluation was performed on the curated multi-source dataset detailed in Section 3, comprising
approximately 3.2 million course entries aggregated from Coursera, Udemy, and edX. The dataset includes a wide
range of course metadata and user interaction logs, covering diverse subject areas and varying difficulty levels
(beginner, intermediate, and advanced). To ensure fair evaluation, the data was split into 80% training and 20% testing
sets using stratified sampling, preserving the distribution of course categories and engagement levels. The proposed
recommendation framework utilized the Learning-to-Rank (LTR) paradigm with XGBoost in pairwise ranking mode
(rank:pairwise objective). A comprehensive hyperparameter tuning process was carried out using 5-fold cross-
validation on the training set. The model was optimized for Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG), which
emphasizes the importance of placing highly relevant courses at the top of the recommendation list. All experimentation
was executed on a workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon 3.6 GHz CPU, 64 GB RAM, and NVIDIA RTX A5000
GPU. The implementation leveraged Python 3.11, scikit‑learn, and the official XGBoost library.

4.3. Experimental Results
      The XGBoost-based Learning-to-Rank (LTR) model was optimized with grid search and 5-fold cross-validation in
order to maximize the NDCG metric on the validation set while maintaining generalization capacity. The model utilized
the rank: pairwise objective, which is specifically designed for pairwise ranking tasks. The learning rate was 0.1 to
allow for gradual convergence, and the maximum tree depth was 8 to achieve a tradeoff between model complexity and
training time. 300 boosting rounds were permitted overall, with early stopping activated if performance stabilized for 20
rounds in a row. Regularization was also added by applying L1             (reg_alpha=0.1) and L2 (reg_lambda=1.0)
penalties. Subsampling techniques row-wise (subsample=0.8) and column-wise (colsample_bytree=0.7) were used to
avoid overfitting. Min_child_weight=1 and gamma=0.2 were also used, which avoid redundant splits by imposing split

constraints. These hyperparameters collectively led to a high-performing, well-regularized model that had excellent

      The proposed Learning-to-Rank (LTR) model integrated with XGBoost demonstrated substantial improvements

4.4. Comparison with Deep Learning Recommender Models
     Recent research in recommendation systems has increasingly explored deep learning–based approaches, including
Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF), DeepFM, and transformer-based ranking models, which are capable of learning
complex nonlinear interactions and latent representations. These models have reported strong performance in offline
evaluation settings, particularly when large volumes of dense user–item interaction data are available.
     However, such deep learning models often incur higher training and inference costs, require extensive
hyperparameter tuning, and may introduce latency challenges when deployed in real-time educational platforms.
Transformer-based ranking systems, in particular, demand significant computational resources, which can limit their
practicality in large-scale learning environments where responsiveness is critical.
     In contrast, the proposed Learning-to-Rank framework using XGBoost achieves competitive ranking performance
while maintaining low query latency and modest computational requirements. As shown in Table 6, the proposed
method attains an NDCG@10 of 0.85 and an MRR of 0.81, which is comparable to or exceeds reported results of neural
collaborative filtering and transformer-based recommenders in similar educational and content-ranking contexts.
     The results indicate that the proposed approach offers a favorable balance between ranking effectiveness and
deployment efficiency. While deep learning recommenders remain promising for capturing rich semantic and
behavioral patterns, the XGBoost-based LTR model provides a practical and scalable alternative for real-time course
recommendation systems. Future work will explore hybrid architectures that combine transformer-based representations
with the proposed ranking framework.

continuously adapt to evolving user behavior, reinforcing its effectiveness in delivering personalized, context-aware
course recommendations.

5. Conclusion and Future Work

      This work presents the implementation of a real-time feedback loop along with the XGBoost algorithm to update
an already defined Learning-to-Rank (LTR) model to dynamically fit changing user requirements. Utilizing a high-
quality dataset collected from top MOOC sites such as Coursera, Udemy, and edX, the system captures and models rich
user-course interactions and signals. Ranking educational content with fine granularity, the system model employs TF-
IDF representation, user profile embeddings, and engagement-based metrics as coarse and context-aware user profiling
methods for real-time multidisciplinary content ranking. Comparison with collaborative filtering and content-based
ranking methods revealed strong quantitative improvement in all the most important ranking measures, demonstrating
the performance of the LTR + XGBoost model. Most notably, the system achieved an NDCG@10 of 0.85, MRR of
0.81, and Precision@10 of 0.83. The integration of the LTR feedback model further enhanced precision, which
demonstrates the system's flexibility. The ability to tailor recommendations on a per-user level, while maintaining a low
latency of 112 ms makes the model appropriate for real-time responsive learning settings. The study emphasizes the
need for advanced ranking algorithms coupled with real-time user engagement feedback for accurate learning
suggestions. The proposed framework tackles course and user engagement improvement in the modifiable and
generalizable setting, presenting applicability within diverse e-learning environments.
      Expanding the cross-linguistic and cross-cultural personalization framework will be the focus of subsequent
research, aiming to better adapt the system to various groups of learners who are from different cultures and languages.
Additionally, the growing popularity of mobile learning necessitates models for mobile and edge applications are
designed to be lightweight and energy-efficient in order to ensure a balanced model of performance and accessibility on
every device. A key limitation of this study is the absence of online evaluation through live deployment or controlled
user studies. While offline metrics provide valuable insight into ranking performance, they do not fully capture user
satisfaction, long-term engagement, or system behavior under real-world traffic conditions. Future work will focus on
deploying the proposed system in a live educational platform to conduct user studies and A/B testing, enabling a more
comprehensive assessment of practical effectiveness.
